Part one — what makes it complete

Everything here exists in messengers this audience already uses. Their absence is what makes Sakina feel like a demo rather than a product. Use the filter to see only what fits the time you have.

Effort

A · Motion and feel

The app has almost no motion today. That is absence, not restraint — the brand says motion should settle, and there is currently nothing to settle. Everything here routes through the reduce-motion path that already exists and is not yet used.

#FeatureWhy it mattersEffort
A1Hero transition into a chatThe avatar and title fly from the row into the app bar. The highest-value animation in any messenger — it answers "where did I come from" without a thought, and it is most of what makes Telegram feel expensive.
A2Send animationThe bubble lifts out of the composer instead of appearing. The most repeated interaction in the product — ten thousand times a day per user, currently a hard cut.
A3Staggered list entryRows arrive over ~200ms on first load. Without it an empty list becoming a full one just pops, and popping reads as a bug.
A4Pull-to-refresh with the chorkhona drawing itselfA brand moment in a gesture people already make. Cheap — the mark is already a CustomPainter, so animating its stroke is a few lines.
A5Animated typing dotsCurrently plain text, which reads as a status bar rather than as a person at the other end.
A6Badge count-down on readClearing a chat deserves feedback. Counting to zero is more satisfying than a badge blinking out.
A7Swipe-to-reply, spring-back, haptic at thresholdThe most-used gesture in modern messengers, and a textbook expert shortcut that costs beginners nothing.
A8Long-press menu with scale-and-blurWhere reply, forward, copy, delete and react all live. Without it every action needs its own permanent button.
A9Reduced-motion enforced by testWe built the zero-duration path and nothing checks that animations use it. A guardrail nobody verifies decays within a month.
A10A haptics vocabularyLight on send, heavier on error, none on scroll. Haptics are the cheapest way to feel expensive and the easiest to overdo; writing the rules down is what stops it.
A11Skeleton shimmer that respects reduce-motionThe shimmer is the part that usually gets the accessibility path wrong.
A12Directional page transitionsForward and back currently use the same builder. Direction is how people build a spatial model of an app.

B · Loading, empty and error states

Not aesthetics — completeness. This is the category that reveals a screen nobody used with slow, absent or broken data.

#FeatureWhy it mattersEffort
B1Skeleton chat list on cold startCurrently an empty screen while SQLite opens — 300–600ms of looking broken on a cheap Android, on every single launch.
B2Skeleton message listWorse than the list: an empty conversation looks like lost history, which is the single scariest thing a messenger can show.
B3Retry on a failed bubbleAlready flagged as owed in the UX doc. A failed message you cannot retry is a dead end with your words in it.
B4Real upload progressWe report 0 then 1. On a 40MB video over a slow uplink that is two minutes of nothing happening. Needs a streaming PUT.
B5Cancel an in-flight uploadSending the wrong 40MB video and being unable to stop it costs real money on metered data.
B6Distinguish no-network from server-unreachableTwo different user actions — turn on wifi, or wait. Conflating them wastes the user's time and trust.
B7Empty states with a next actionNo members, no results, no media. Each needs something to do, not a shrug.
B8First-run experienceA new account currently sees an empty list and a button. There is no moment that explains what this app is or why it exists.
B9Error taxonomy in three languagesServer-side messages are human; some client paths still surface raw strings, in one language.

C · Profile and settings

The biggest structural hole. There is no profile screen and no settings screen at all — language is a sheet hanging off the app bar and sign-out is an icon next to it. Telegram's settings tree is the reference because it is the one this audience already has in their heads.

If I had to pick ten

First stop the app feeling broken. Then stop it feeling empty. Only then make it worth switching to.

  1. Reply — group chat is unreadable without it
  2. Settings, theme and device list — the app has nowhere to be configured
  3. Thumbnails — the largest unnecessary data cost in the product
  4. Voice messages — the primary input method for a large share of the audience
  5. Hero and send animations — the two motions that make it feel built rather than assembled
  6. Skeleton screens — 600ms of looking broken on every cold start
  7. Notification grouping — the current behaviour is an uninstall trigger
  8. Usernames and profile QR — kills "paste a user id" without waiting for contact discovery
  9. Block and report — safety floor, and a store-review requirement
  10. The data budget — the cheapest thing that makes us visibly different

Part two — what makes it chosen

The pigeon question

There is a genre of messaging app built on artificial delay. The serious one is Slowly, where a letter travels at a simulated speed based on the real geographic distance between two people — Dushanbe to Moscow takes hours. There are novelty versions where a cartoon pigeon carries the message and occasionally dies, losing it.

These work, and it is worth being precise about why, because copying the surface would be a mistake.

The mechanic is manufactured scarcity. Instant messaging made a message cost nothing to send, and things that cost nothing to send stop being worth much to receive. A pen-pal app puts the cost back: you wait, so you write something worth waiting for. The delay is not the feature — the care the delay forces is the feature. The pigeon dying is the same trick amplified: a message that might not arrive is a message you choose your words for.

The thing that changes everything

Sakina's users do not need scarcity manufactured for them. They already have it. A construction worker in Yekaterinburg with 200MB left in the month and a two-hour gap to his daughter in Kulob is living inside the exact constraint Slowly simulates. Selling him simulated distance would be, at best, tone-deaf.

So the move is not to copy the gimmick. It is to invert it:

Instant messengers treat distance as a problem to erase. Our users cannot erase it. The opportunity is to make real distance felt as connection rather than as absence — to design for the separation instead of pretending it away.

That reframe is defensible in a way a pigeon is not, because it comes from the audience rather than from a mood board. Every Tier 1 idea below falls out of it.

Six tests for a candidate

An idea failing 4, 5 or 6 should be cut regardless of how good it sounds in a pitch.

1Survives 2G and a 16GB phone?If it needs bandwidth we do not have, it is a feature for a different market.
2Would someone describe it?At 5–10k users word of mouth is the only channel. A feature nobody can explain in one sentence never gets explained.
3Better with a whole family?Group value beats solo value. The unit of adoption is a household, not a person.
4How fast could Telegram copy it?If a sprint, it is not a moat — though still worth building for the users we get first.
5Is it quiet?The chrome stays still so the content can be loud. A feature that shouts is off-brief however clever.
6Is it honest?Does it solve a problem the user has, or manufacture one so we have something to solve? This is the pigeon test, and it is the one most novelty features fail.

Tier 1 — distinctive and load-bearing

1

Нома — the letter

A second message mode, built on removing signals rather than adding them

You compose a long message over hours or days, kept as a local draft, and send it once. It arrives as a letter — its own view, its own typography, an opening ritual. Deliberately missing: typing indicators, read receipts, last-seen, and any pressure to reply quickly. A letter is answered with a letter, days later, and that is the expected rhythm rather than a failure state.

Why

This is Slowly's real insight applied to people who actually live it. A migrant worker gets twenty minutes on a Sunday. Chat's rhythm punishes that — he opens the app to forty unanswered messages and a week of accumulated guilt. Nothing in a chat interface ever says it is fine that you were away. A letter's rhythm says exactly that: nobody feels bad about taking three days to answer a letter.

We would be removing the pressure instant messaging created, for the people it hurts most. And it inverts the pigeon precisely — no artificial delay, no bird. The delay is the one already in these people's lives, and the app stops treating it as a fault.

Cheap, too: a message type, a different renderer, and suppressing signals we already send.

Against it

Two modes is a concept to teach, and concepts are expensive. It risks being used once and forgotten. Mitigation: do not launch it as a mode — launch it as what happens when someone writes past a few hundred characters, and let the app offer it.

2

Ҳастам — the presence beacon

Roughly fifty bytes, sent by one tap

A signal, not a message: I'm home. I'm fine. I've arrived. Good night. It shows as a state against your name, to the handful of people who care. No text, no thread, no reply expected.

Why

The deepest anxiety in a split family is not a shortage of conversation. It is not knowing. Did he get there. Is she alright. Is the bus in. A call to ask costs money, and a message asking "are you okay?" carries an implication nobody wants to make explicit.

WhatsApp's "last seen" accidentally serves this need — which is why people watch it obsessively and why it feels like surveillance. It is information leaked rather than offered. The same information, offered deliberately and mutually, stops being surveillance and becomes care. That reframing is the whole idea.

It is also the cheapest thing anyone can send on a metered connection, which matters strategically: a reason to prefer us that is created by the very constraint our competitors ignore.

Against it

Presence becomes obligation — "you didn't beacon me" is a real failure mode, and in a controlling relationship it is considerably worse than that. It must be per-person, silently revocable, and must never show a history. Design this one with someone who understands coercive control before writing any code.

3

The data budget, on the surface

A number, not a toggle

34 of 200 MB this month. Per-chat breakdown. A projection of what the video you are about to send will cost. One tap for "text only until payday."

Why

For this audience data is money and the anxiety is constant and specific. No major messenger surfaces it, because their users genuinely do not care — which is exactly why it is available to us. It converts our hardest constraint into our most visible advantage, and it is explainable in one sentence, which at this size is the whole game.

A second-order effect worth naming: a user who can see the cost of media sends less of it, which lowers our storage and egress bill. The interests line up, which is rare enough to be worth noticing.

Against it

Accurate per-chat attribution is fiddly, and an approximate number that is visibly wrong is worse than no number at all.

4

Пайк — carry a message for a stranger

Store-and-forward over Bluetooth. A research spike, not a feature

Your phone holds an encrypted, sealed message for someone else and hands it on when it meets a phone with signal. Prior art: Briar, Bridgefy, FireChat.

Why

There are places in this country where this is the only thing that works — Pamir valleys, the Anzob tunnel, the border queue at Oybek where thousands of people wait for hours with no signal and one thing on their minds, which is telling someone at home where they are.

And it is a story. "The app that works in the mountains" is the kind of sentence that gets repeated — test 2 in its strongest form. The Pamirs are also exactly where the chorkhona in our own logo comes from, which is a coherence you cannot buy.

Against it

The hardest thing on this page by a wide margin. Battery cost, an entirely separate trust and encryption model — you are asking phones to carry strangers' data — abuse potential, and platform restrictions on background Bluetooth that tighten with every OS release. Scope it as a spike, and probably as a separate optional capability rather than something in the main path.

Tier 2 — culturally specific, low cost, high affinity

None of these carries the product alone. Each one signals this was built for you, by someone who knows — and here that signal is worth more than a feature comparison.

5 · Namoz-aware quiet

Prayer times computed on-device from location — no network call, no location leaving the phone. Notifications hold during the window and deliver after. Optionally a shared quiet state, so a family knows why someone is not replying.

Millions of people in this audience pray five times a day and every messenger they use is oblivious to it. It is also literally what the app's name means: sakina is tranquility, and holding the phone still during prayer is that name taken seriously. Must be opt-in, and the calculation method must be right — there are several conventions, and a wrong time is worse than no feature.

6 · Time-zone-aware sending

Sakina knows the recipient's local time. Sending at 02:00 their time offers: deliver now, or at 07:00? Dushanbe–Moscow is two hours; Dushanbe–Vladivostok is five the other way. Waking a parent at 2am is a real and frequent harm and every messenger makes it effortless. The cheapest idea on this page, and one of the kindest.

7 · Navruz and Eid time capsules

Write now, delivered on the day. Navruz is the moment of the year in this culture and when the diaspora most wants to reach home. A message written in a quiet February evening and delivered on 21 March is a better message than one typed in a rush on the morning. This is the pigeon's anticipation mechanic, honestly earned — the delay is chosen by the sender, for a reason, rather than imposed by a cartoon bird.

8 · Elder mode

A whole-app switch: larger targets, voice-first composition, three things on screen instead of thirty, no swipe gestures. A very large share of the people being called are grandparents in villages, often on a first smartphone. Every messenger fails them, and the person who installs the app is usually a grandchild who will choose whichever one their grandmother can actually use.

9 · Тоҷикӣ keyboard help

The six characters ғ ӣ қ ӯ ҳ ҷ are absent from most default Cyrillic keyboards, so people substitute Russian letters and their own language degrades on screen. Even a long-press row above the composer would be notable. It costs almost nothing, and it is upstream of a commitment we have already made — our fonts are chosen on those six characters, so rendering them beautifully while leaving people unable to type them would be strange.

Tier 3 — worth exploring, unproven

  • On-device voice transcription. Send text instead of audio when bandwidth is bad; make voice notes searchable. Russian ASR is good enough today; Tajik is not, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest — treat Tajik as research, not a feature.
  • Remittance receipts. The obvious payments on-ramp, and the message type is already reserved. Needs a licensed partner and years of regulatory work.
  • Village and mahalla channels. Strong community fit, serious moderation liability. Do not ship without the report queue.
  • Shared family album. A group's photos as an album rather than a scroll. Competes with the message list for attention, so it must not become a feed.

What we should not build

Being explicit about rejections is most of the value of a document like this.

A literal pigeonCute once, uninstalled twice. Fails test 6 outright — it manufactures a problem for people who have the real version. Also the loudest possible chrome, so it fails test 5 as well.
Messages that can be lost on purposeThe pigeon's death mechanic. Our entire protocol — gapless sequence numbers, idempotent retries, offline catch-up — exists to make delivery trustworthy on a network that is already unreliable. Deliberately losing a message would be undoing our best engineering as a joke, in a market where "did it arrive?" is a daily anxiety.
Stories or a feedTelegram and WhatsApp both have them and we lose that race on content volume. It also directly contradicts a quiet app so the conversation can be loud.
An AI assistant in the chat listEvery messenger is doing it, it costs real money per user, it needs a network round trip this audience cannot afford, and it answers no question anyone here is asking. On-device transcription is different — that is a tool, not a personality.
Crypto, tokens, NFTsThe payments story here is remittances and utility bills through a licensed partner. Anything else is regulatory suicide in a country whose National Bank is not relaxed about this.
Privacy as the headlineBuild disappearing messages — they are expected. But positioning on privacy invites a comparison with Signal that we lose, and a conversation with a government we would rather have later than sooner.
Gamification — streaks, points, badgesDuolingo's mechanics applied to a family conversation would be grotesque. A streak turns talking to your mother into a chore with a score attached.
A crowded super-app grid on day oneThe super-app is the destination, and the mini-app grid is what it looks like after people already open the app daily. Shipping the grid first is how you get an app that does eleven things badly.

The one-sentence version

If Sakina is going to be described by one person to another, the sentence should be something like:

It's the one that works when you have no signal and no data left, and it doesn't make you feel bad for not replying.

Which is Tier 1, items 1 through 4. Everything else is support.

What to do with this now

None of it, yet. The ten items above come first. But two things are worth doing immediately, because they are cheap and they inform everything else:

  1. Talk to twenty people in the corridor — ten in Russia, ten at home. Ask what they do when they run out of data, and what they do when they cannot reach someone. Do not ask what features they want; nobody can answer that. Ideas 1, 2 and 3 are hypotheses drawn from published market facts and they need contact with real people before anyone writes code.
  2. Reserve the protocol space. Adding letter and beacon to the message-type enum costs nothing today and is awkward to retrofit later — the same reasoning that already put payment and service_card in there before a line of payments code existed.
