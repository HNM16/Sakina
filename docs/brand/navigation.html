Why this is Flutter's transition and not ours

The slide is four lines of arithmetic. The gesture is not, and the gesture is the part people feel.

Dragging back has to take the route animation over from its controller mid-flight, run linearly while the finger is down — a curve applied to a drag is the thing that makes a swipe feel like it is arguing with you — hand control back on release, decide by velocity or by position which end to settle at, and fly the avatar to whichever screen wins. Flutter already ships that, correct, and it installs from the same builder on every platform. The gesture was never iOS-only; only the habit of registering the builder on iOS was.

So SakinaPageTransitions delegates to CupertinoPageTransitionsBuilder and keeps exactly one thing of its own: the reduce-motion gate. That is not a small keep. Registering Cupertino raw is what iOS and macOS did until now, and Cupertino has no reduce-motion path — so the platform whose users are likeliest to have the setting switched on was the one platform ignoring it.

Guardrail G10 — convention

docs/UX.md copies Telegram's chrome because familiarity is free adoption, and this is that argument at its strongest: navigation is the one interaction where being surprising is only ever a cost.

The corner and the edge

A gesture nobody has discovered yet is not a way back.

So there are two, as in Telegram: an arrow in the leading corner of the bar, and the edge drag. The arrow is written out rather than left to the app bar's automatic one, because the automatic one takes its tooltip from MaterialLocalizations — and Flutter ships no Tajik, so a phone set to Tajik would be told Назад. Ours says Бозгашт.

The edge costs something, and it is worth saying which: a swipe-to-reply that starts in the leftmost 20 px is now a back gesture instead. Both are horizontal drags on the same bubble, so one of them has to yield, and leaving it to the gesture arena resolves it by tree depth — meaning reply wins and the back swipe silently does not work. Telegram gives the edge to navigation. So do we.

Under reduce-motion

The transition disappears entirely: the route swaps with nothing moving, because shortening an animation is not disabling it. But a drag that follows your finger is the motion that was turned off, and losing a way out of a screen is not what anyone asked for when they turned it off. So the edge keeps working as a discrete fling — over the threshold and the screen is simply gone, under it and nothing happened. Switch Motion → Reduced above and try the strip.
