Сакина сукунат — stillness

A quiet app so the conversation can be loud.

Every messenger in this market shouts. Ours is named for tranquility, and it should behave like it.

The mark is a chorkhona — the stepped skylight of a Pamiri house, four squares each turned against the one below, opening to let light into the home. It is the right shape for an app whose main job is keeping families in touch across a border, and four straight-edged shapes cost a budget phone almost nothing to paint. Small is where it does not hold up yet: under about 32 pixels the strokes merge, so the launcher icon still needs a solid-fill variant.

The ground

Lapis night, firuza tile

Dark first, and not for fashion. It is what an OLED-less budget Android spends least battery on, what stays readable in mountain sun, and what a word meaning stillness actually looks like. Light is a complete, equally-designed swap — not an afterthought inversion.

Firuza is the glaze on Samanid tilework — the dynasty Tajik national identity is built on. It is ours in a way a flag colour is not, and it is nobody else's in this category.

Ruled out

What we are deliberately not

Telegram blue

The incumbent's colour. Adopting it reads as imitation, and we are asking people to switch, not to squint.

WhatsApp green

Also the instinctive "Islamic green". It is the most-used messenger in Tajikistan — the one colour guaranteed to be misread as a clone.

The flag palette

A state messenger already launched here. Wearing the flag invites exactly the comparison we need to avoid.

Atlas stripes as chrome

The rainbow ikat silk is the most recognisable Tajik pattern there is — and unreadable behind text. It belongs in stickers, not the UI.

Type

Six letters decide the font

Tajik Cyrillic adds characters that plain Russian fonts do not carry. Pick a face without them and the app falls back mid-word — the fastest possible way to look foreign. Every candidate gets tested on these six first. The specimen below is live: if you can see the macrons on Ӣ and Ӯ and the descenders on Қ, Ҳ and Ҷ, the face you are reading this in has them.

Ғ ғU+0492
Ӣ ӣU+04E2
Қ қU+049A
Ӯ ӯU+04EE
Ҳ ҳU+04B2
Ҷ ҷU+04B6

Interface — Noto Sans

Chosen because coverage is its entire reason for existing. Ships with Android, so no download on a metered connection, and it renders all six without falling back.

Numbers — tabular

Timestamps, unread counts, and one day payment amounts sit in columns. Lining figures stop them shifting as they tick.

Sentence case everywhere. Tajik and Russian both read badly in ALL CAPS, and shouting is off-brief.

The interface

Telegram's shape, our temperature

The layout is familiar on purpose: chat list, thread, composer at the bottom, mine on the right. Familiarity is free adoption, and every gratuitous difference is a tax paid by someone deciding whether to switch. What changes is the temperature — softer contrast, one accent instead of five, and space where other apps put dividers.

Рӯз — bright sun, low battery

Rules

Five things that keep it ours

01
One accent, and it is firuza.

Saffron is for rare warmth — a Navruz greeting, a pinned chat. Pomegranate appears only when something is lost or destroyed. A screen with three accent colours has none.

02
The chrome is quiet so the content can be loud.

Stickers, photos and voice notes carry the colour and the humour. The frame around them stays still.

03
Motion settles, never bounces.

Short, soft easing under 200ms. No springs, no overshoot. It reads as calm and it costs a budget phone nothing — the aesthetic and the constraint agree.

04
Space instead of lines.

Separate groups with room, not dividers. Fewer strokes means less to render and less to look at.

05
Tajik decides the layout, not English.

Tajik strings run roughly a third longer than their English equivalents. Every button is sized against the Tajik word first, or the UI breaks in the only language that matters.
