ЧАРХcharkh — the turn

The mark turns, holds, and turns again.

Three numbers describe the whole thing: how far it turns, how long that takes, and how long it waits before the next one.

How far matters more than it looks. The bars are a pinwheel, so a multiple of 90° puts the mark back on its own silhouette — it stops somewhere it has already been, and reads as arriving rather than as spinning. Anything else leaves it resting at an angle it has never rested at before, which is a different feeling entirely and worth trying.

The fourth number is not timing. Blur is a shutter: the mark is smeared across the angle it sweeps in that many milliseconds, so it softens while it is moving fastest and is perfectly sharp the moment it stops. Nothing about when or how far it turns depends on it — at 0 ms the animation is identical, just hard-edged. Past about 40 ms the mark dissolves into a ring.

Four bars, one colour, firuza at #32BBC8. Nothing else moves and nothing changes colour.

The blur is real angular blur — the mark drawn many times across the arc it sweeps and accumulated, not a soft filter laid over the top. The number of copies follows how far the outer corner actually travels, so a 20 px mark costs a handful and a 96 px one costs a couple of dozen.

Under reduced motion the mark stands still and a word carries the state, because an indicator that neither moves nor says anything is not an indicator. Nothing here has been seen on a handset.
