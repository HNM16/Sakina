Starting, turning, and finishing.

The gaps are the whole argument. Four bars laid in a weave with the joints still open is a wall that is not finished — that is why the mark reads as working rather than as a logo sitting there. So the way to say done is to close them: the bricks slide up against each other, the gaps disappear, and what is left is a solid square ring that has nothing left to do.

Which makes the opening obvious too. At rest the mark is closed. Work begins by breaking the joints open. Same gesture in reverse, so the two ends of the loading state are one idea rather than two.

One rule holds the finish together: the mark always lands square. The close finishes whatever turn is in flight and only then locks. Stopping mid-turn and closing would read as interrupted, which is the opposite of what this has to say.
